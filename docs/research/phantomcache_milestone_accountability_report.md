## 0. Headline (read this first)
1. **No milestone has passed its original acceptance criteria in an independently verified way.** M1–M8 were all *proposed* packages; no document records a written owner authorization of each package or a dated owner sign-off against its AC list (reconstruction below). Several pass/fail statuses in the master plan were AG-reported and later reopened by audit.
2. **The engine is substantially stronger than at the start**: ten correctness or security defects were found by independent review and fixed with failing-before tests (S-1, S-2, S-9, H1/H2 304/sweeper/fencing, idle-CPU spin, S-3 replica coverage, request-line injection, anonymous cache reads, unauthenticated intelligence endpoints, permissive CORS, client-header cache poisoning), and the debug test baseline rose from the reported 242 to 774 passing tests at the last full lead-run [L].
3. **The headline research ambition was tested and not supported.** The sealed M6 evaluation (Tier 1, simulation) gave H1 NOT_SUPPORTED; Scenario B on the real gateway (loopback, 30 runs) shows the origin cap trades client success for lower origin concurrency (TRADE_OFF_FAILURE for both capped arms). **MVP-B is the provisional product direction.** The first M6 "MVP-A" report was fabricated by arithmetic literals and is retracted.
4. **The product has never been looked at in a browser by this project.** M8 is implemented and unit-tested, not verified. The Lab HTTP API (D-4) and the Phantom Lab UI (D-6) do not exist yet.
5. **Percentage complete: not defensible.** The milestones are not commensurable, acceptance criteria were never frozen per package, and half of the credible evidence is "implemented, tested, not independently verified". A number would be invented. Section 8 gives the verdict by level instead.

### 11.3 What the AIMD evaluation demonstrated, and what it did not
Demonstrated (data or code):
1. **The tested controller behaved as a threshold switch.** [L] AIMD starts at the maximum (`candidates.rs:149-150`), compares measured origin p99 with an **absolute** ceiling and decreases by 30% (`:155-166`); the error trigger is hard-coded at 0.05 (`:156`), so the tuned `pie_error_threshold` never reaches AIMD. 2. **The window "p99" is the window maximum for any window <= 99 samples** [L, `executor.rs:147-151`, nearest-rank `ceil(0.99 n)`], so one outlier triggers a 30% cut. Scenario B's AIMD arm (window 30, ceiling 60 ms vs healthy origin p50 ~28 ms and timer overshoot up to 29 ms) sat at its floor from the first second [A: snapshots of rep01].

An adaptive controller that starts at max cannot beat a static low cap on a "peak" statistic, so H1 as worded was close to a foregone conclusion. 4. **Scenario B's plant cannot show origin protection**: the scripted origin is a pure delay server (`lab_live/origin.rs`), origin p99 service moved 143.8 -> 142.4 ms with the AIMD cap; any binding cap there only sheds. Latency "wins" are survivorship (fast 503s): 200-only P99 is unchanged [A, one run]. By Little's law (about 182 origin requests/s x 0.12 s ~ 22 concurrent in the degraded phase) cap 24 sits just above mean demand and cap 12-15 sheds heavily [lead-reviewed arithmetic, not a computed Erlang-B figure].
5. Methodology limits: 28 s horizon vs TTL 3 s so the dynamic stale window cannot bind (cell-identical arms 1200/1200); quantized/saturated P99 (identical across arms in 5 of 8 scenarios); one cache size; asymmetric tuning (6-parameter coordinate search of 24-32 evaluations vs exhaustive static grid); adaptivity constraint added after seeing T and D (disclosed); feasibility judged on cross-scenario means (per-scenario harm averaged away); T1 never validated against T2 (C7).

**Net statement the evidence supports:** one controller design (absolute-ceiling AIMD started at max, scored on peak concurrency) failed under an ill-posed objective on two plants that cannot reward it. ## 12. M3 invalidation durability: investigation result (added 2026-10-30 07:10 +05:30; supersedes the M3 wording in sections 2 and 9 where they differ)
Basis: read-only investigation (agent), key claims re-read by the lead: `src/main.rs` contains no `router.stop()`/drain call (grep: only `begin_draining()` at lines 481/485, the exit message "cache is in-memory, nothing to flush" at 486, and `rt.shutdown_timeout(5 s)` at 177); in `delete_cache_handler` the local `cache.delete` precedes the awaited cluster call (`server.rs:1952`, `:1957`). Not reproduced (no test exists); confidence in the mechanism ~90%.
**Classification:** (1) **Confirmed by code reading, medium severity, not yet reproduced: invalidations accepted (204) but not yet acknowledged by every peer exist only in the issuing gateway's process memory** (broadcaster channel, per-peer worker queues and in-flight job, RepairLedger, stream sequencers, replay ring); a crash, kill or SIGTERM loses them and a peer that missed one serves the stale entry until it expires. (2) **Documentation overclaim, confirmed:** ADR-003 section 4.1 lists "Node Restarts: supported via clean sequence resync and cold-start catchup", G2/G3 omit the "while the issuing gateway stays alive" qualifier that the code comment at `router.rs:1786-1789` states; `docs/user/limitations.md:8` is stale; `operate.md:33` does not warn about peer staleness after a restart. (3) **Missing validation test:** nothing kills or restarts the issuer with a non-empty ledger; the harness has a public `child`/`start_kill` but no restart API (command, env and ports are not retained). (4) **Architectural limitation:** in-memory, no-consensus design (documented as deferred in phase-3/4 design). (5) **Possible, unconfirmed:** whether a quiet issuer restart is ever noticed by peers through other paths.
**Failure sequence (kill -9):** client mutates origin and sends DELETE /k to G; G purges locally, gets the primary's synchronous ack (150 ms leg), hands other peers to the async queue and returns 204; delivery to peer B is slow/blocked (up to ~1.4 s of retries, then the ledger with 250 ms repair ticks; minutes if B is partitioned/Suspect); G dies; G restarts with empty ledger, ring, sequence counters back at 1 and all peers Active; B still serves /k. **Graceful SIGTERM:** the same loss, deterministically for anything queued (no drain; tasks cancelled at exit); `WorkerGuard` drains run only on `stop()` or worker panic and only into the same memory.
**Why peers do not recover:** the receiver applies every InvalidateRequest regardless of sequence (so a restarted sender's *new* invalidations work) but cannot detect a restarted sender (new stream seq 1 vs expected N+1 is ignored as "duplicate or obsolete"); pulls happen only when the peer is Suspect/Recovering or has gaps; pool heartbeats do not change node state. If a pull does happen, an empty sender ring yields HistoryOverwritten, i.e. the requester flushes its whole cache (safe but coarse); the old debts can never be reconstructed. Recovery otherwise: expiry, or a later DELETE of the same key.
**Impact:** only keys whose DELETE was in the delivery window (or in the ledger) and only peers that had not acknowledged; only those keys' tenants. Duration = remaining freshness lifetime: default effective TTL is a literal 60 s (`PHANTOM_DEFAULT_TTL_MS` unwired; `server.rs:1367,1650`), but **origin `max-age`/`s-maxage`/`Expires` has no gateway ceiling** (`freshness.rs:267-274`) and `PHANTOM_MAX_STALE_MS` caps only the stale window, so staleness can be as long as the origin allows plus up to 24 h of stale-if-error: ADR-003 G3 "staleness <= TTL_eff <= PHANTOM_MAX_STALE_MS" is an overclaim. A degraded window (a peer down during a rolling deploy) is exactly when the risk peaks. For deletion/GDPR-style removal a 204 does not prove the object is gone from every gateway.
**Other gaps found:** DELETE handler cancellation (client disconnect may drop the handler future after the local purge and before any debt is recorded: hypothesis, axum behaviour unverified; fix = run the cluster leg detached); 504 paths record debts only in memory; static membership; no passive restart detection; ADR says anti-entropy every 5 s vs code 250 ms; no TTL ceiling; false gaps after sequence aliasing.
**Remediation options (ranked):** (1) correct the documentation now (ADR-003 4.1/G2/G3, limitations.md, operate.md, claims ledger C6 qualifier) - mandatory, no code risk; (2) graceful-shutdown drain with a deadline (stop accepting DELETE, run broadcaster and repair until the ledger is empty or ~2-5 s, then stop) - ~50-100 lines, planned restarts only; (3) **opt-in durable owed-log** (env-gated path, off by default): append-only intent record per accepted DELETE before 204 with per-peer ack watermark/checkpoints, replay into the existing bounded ledger on boot, group-commit fsync, compaction when all peers have acked, corrupt/missing log after an unclean exit falls back to a full-flush debt for every peer - ~400-700 lines, medium risk; (4) boot-id in frames for fast restart detection and tracker reset (wire-format change; detection only, cannot recover keys without a log; full-flush-on-boot is rejected as cluster-wide cold cache); (5) delayed-204/strict mode as an opt-in (ADR-003 rejected it as default); (6) TTL ceiling and short `s-maxage` guidance for deletion-sensitive content. **Not to build:** consensus or replicated invalidation logs, persisting ring/sequence counters alone, flush-all-peers on every boot, persisting the cache.
**Stated consistency model to adopt:** 204 means the issuer purged locally, the primary acknowledged, and every other member WILL be invalidated provided the issuer does not crash before delivery (and, if the durable log is enabled, provided its log survives); after an issuer crash without the log the guarantee degrades to expiry. Failure model: process crash/restart and partition; excludes disk loss. **Required tests:** ledger round-trip incl. flush markers and in-flight/again flags, truncated-last-record tolerance, replay size bound, corrupt-log fallback; multi-process kill -9 with a non-empty ledger then restart (peer converges within the repair bound), kill right after 204, SIGTERM drain with backlog, partition + restart, concurrent DELETE + restart, a negative control with no persistence documenting the limitation; harness: per-node kill/restart helpers (~40-60 lines). Any change touching invalidation needs an independent review and a failing-before test (CLAUDE.md section 5) and owner approval for application-code changes.
