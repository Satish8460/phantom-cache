## 0. Summary and recommendation

- **What was measured (reproduced here by script on the current sources):** 131,712 words in the sources (whitespace tokens; front matter 764, 16 chapter/appendix h1 units, 205 h2 sections in the sources plus 5 in the front matter = 210 h2 headings, which matches `grep -c '^## '` on the assembled Markdown (210); I could not reproduce the figure of 234 h2 sections; 318 h3 sections); the lead's figure is 131,172 words (I measure 131,712; I did not reconcile the difference of 540) and 183 pages (about 717 words per page), and I use 183 pages scaled to my total for page estimates. 33 figures. 93 judge boxes in the chapters plus a 12,779-word Appendix E that restates them (93 entries).
- **Judge material is the largest single block:** chapter judge boxes 20,322 words + Appendix E 13,693 words = 34,015 words, 26% of the book. Inside the boxes, "If the judge asks further" is 4,469 words (22% of a box), "Suggested answer" 10,683 (53%), "Evidence to know" 1,657 (8%).
- **Proposed split (two layers, nothing deleted):** Part I Learning Handbook **76,113 words (about 106 A4 pages, 58%)**; Part II Technical Reference **55,599 words (about 77 pages)**. 22 chapter sections or appendices (65 h1/h2 units) move whole, 68 of 93 judge boxes move (23 stay), 1,814 words of tables are extracted from 9 tables in 6 sections.
- **True duplicates found: very few.** The only cell-for-cell duplicate is the Chapter 8, 8.12 matrix (44 of 44 rows, 568 words) which Appendix G carries with an extra pointer column. Everything else that looks redundant (Appendix E vs the judge boxes, Appendix B vs Chapter 2, 4.11 and 5.15 vs 8.12 / G) is **paraphrased, not copied**, so removing it would be rewriting, not trimming. I propose no removal beyond the 8.12 table, and only as an owner decision.
- **Recommendation (three sentences).** Adopt the two-layer structure as a build-time view of the existing sources (no rewrite): Part I at about 76,113 words keeps every chapter, worked example, figure, knowledge check and the 23 key judge boxes, and Part II keeps everything else verbatim. This leaves the reader a first book of roughly 106 pages (42% smaller than today) without deleting a word, but that is a reading order, not yet a concise book: a markedly shorter Part I needs a separate editing pass over the chapters and the paraphrased duplicates (about 16,256 words of upper-bound savings, section 7.3), which I have not done. Do it only after the lead has inspected the two prototype PDFs and the 10 cross-references that cross the layer boundary have been given a "Part II" marker.

## 1. Method, and what this analysis is not

1. The sources in `docs/learning/src/` (front matter, 8 chapters, 8 appendices) were parsed read-only. A section is an h1/h2 unit; judge boxes (`::: judge`) are extracted as separate items; headings inside code fences or fenced divs are ignored. Section ids are `chNN:X.Y` (numbered h2), `chNN:slug` (unnumbered h2: What you will learn, Key ideas, Knowledge check, ...), `chNN` / `appX` (the h1 with its introduction). Appendix D (glossary) has no h2 and is one unit; Appendix E has nine thematic h2 sections plus two front h2s.
2. **Word counts are whitespace tokens** (including headings, table pipes counted as tokens only where separated by spaces, code and callouts). The builder's check uses alphanumeric tokens only (so '#', '|', ':::' do not count); both are reported.
3. **Classification is by judgement**, assisted by element statistics: I read the headings of every section, read the opening of roughly 40 sections in full or in part, and measured table, judge-box and callout words for all of them. I did **not** read all 131,000 words. A category is therefore a reasoned proposal, not a verified fact; "A" in particular means "teaches something a newcomer needs", which is a judgement the owner may weigh differently.
4. Categories: **A** essential explanation; **B** detailed technical reference (long tables, route/config, evidence-to-know lists); **C** repeated explanation or redundant table; **D** excessive judge follow-up ("If the judge asks further"); **E** discrepancy / evidence inventory. A section has one category; word-level totals in 3.2 additionally split judge boxes into their parts (D = the follow-up part).
5. Page estimates scale words by 183 / 131,712. Tables and figures probably take more space per word than prose, so the Reference layer (table-heavy) will likely come out at **more** pages than its word share suggests and Part I at fewer; I did not measure per-section pages (needs the PDF's page index, not examined).

## 7. Honest assessment of the two-layer structure

### 7.1 Benefits
- **A reader can finish the first layer.** At about 76,113 words (106 pages; 6.3 h at 200 wpm) Part I is a 42% smaller object than the book, without removing one word from the corpus.
- **The reference material is where a reference reader looks.** Route, configuration, threat-matrix, glossary and source-map tables are consulted by search, not read in order. They interrupt the teaching flow today (for example the 1,814 extracted table words, and 6.11 alone is about 1,430 table words).
- **The status information is stated once per layer, not three times.** Capability labels live today in 4.11, 5.15, 8.12 and Appendix G; in the proposal they all sit together in R1 (and the 8.12 / G duplicate can be reduced to one).
- **Judge defence stays intact.** The ten hard questions' boxes and one core box per chapter stay in the learning layer; every other box is one step away in R4 under the same heading.
- **It is a build-time view.** The sources stay as they are; the builder routes by `layer_map.json`. Reverting the proposal is deleting a folder.

### 7.2 Risks and costs
1. **Cross-references.** I counted 111 section pointers in the Part I text with a regex (approximate); 10 of them (9%) point to sections that would now be in Part II (most-cited: 6.13 (5), 1.10 (1), 2.11 (1), 4.12 (1), 8.7 (1), App E (1)). Appendix E has 93 "Where taught: Ch N, N.M" pointers; I did not check each one; the ones that target a section now in Part II are among the counted cross-layer pointers only if they are written in the Part I text, so the Appendix E pointers (all inside Part II) were not counted. They are not rewritten by the prototype. A real adoption needs "(Part II)" markers on those 10 pointers, and the front-matter "How the chapters are organised" and "Three ways to read it" lists need rewriting.
2. **The judge boxes carry the defence value.** Moving 68 of 93 boxes out of the chapters means a student who reads only Part I meets 23. The mitigation is the stub at the end of each section ("N further judge boxes for this section are in Part II, R4") and the choice of which 23 stay. If the owner thinks a judge question belongs next to the explanation (the style guide's step 7, "Prepare to defend it"), the teaching pattern is weakened by the move. Alternative: keep all boxes in Part I but drop "If the judge asks further" (that edits content and is **not** done here).
3. **Chapters 4 to 6 teach through worked examples and must stay.** They do: the `::: example` blocks (3,002 words, mostly in 4 and 5) are in teaching sections that are not moved. The only Chapter 4 to 6 sections moved are 4.0, 4.11, 4.12, 5.15, 6.11 and 6.13 (labels, status tables, discrepancy list, matrix, measurement inventory); each is an inventory, not a lesson. 6.11 is the largest risk: it is the one table a judge-prep reader may want next to 6.2 to 6.10; it is flagged for owner decision.
4. **Knowledge checks and exploration tasks** are all in Part I (they are not touched); Chapter 4's check answers are in a separate `Answers` section that stays with the chapter.
5. **Two PDFs instead of one** mean two tables of contents, two page numberings and a "which book has it" question. A single-file ordering "Part I, then Part II" avoids this if the owner prefers one PDF; the prototype can concatenate the two Markdown files (not built).
6. **Classification is judgement.** Different owners will move 8.4, 8.2 and 3.1 differently; the whole mechanism is one JSON file so that it is cheap to change.
7. **Not asked, not done:** shortening any text. The proposal reduces what the reader meets first, but total corpus size is unchanged (131k words); an owner who meant "the whole book is too big" has been given a reading order, not a smaller book.

### 7.3 What a later merge pass could save (not done; an editing task for the owner to approve)
- Appendix E re-expressed as pointers plus the Never-say table: up to about 12,813 words if only the pointer lines, question text and the "Never say" list were kept (this would discard the condensed answers; one canonical answer per question would then live only in the chapter box).
- The 8 duplicated question pairs in App E and the same-topic boxes in different chapters (for example "tests pass", "actual intelligence", "DELETE 204", "linearizable", "partitioned mutex"): choose one box, point to it from the others.
- Appendix B (about 3,400 words) merged into Chapter 2 or dropped in favour of Appendix A plus Chapter 2.
These figures are upper bounds computed from section sizes, not tested savings.
- Chapter condensing (not estimated): Chapters 4 to 7 are the largest chapters (13k, 17k, 15k and 13k words); condensing them is the only route to a Part I well below the figure above.

## 8. Recommended structure

**Part I, Learning Handbook (76,113 words, ~106 pages, ~6.3 h reading):** front matter and the reading routes; Chapters 1 to 8 as they are, minus the sections listed in 4.1 and 4.2, with 23 judge boxes; each chapter's Key ideas, What you should now understand, Knowledge check (and answers), exploration tasks and challenge questions; Appendix A (one page) and Appendix H (ten concepts and self-test).

**Part II, Technical Reference (55,599 words, ~77 pages, consulted):** R1 matrices, route and configuration tables (status tables 4.11, 5.15, 8.12 or Appendix G, 8.10, 6.11, the dependency and data-structure tables, Appendix C, plus extracted tables); R2 source-file map (App F); R3 glossary (App D); R4 judge bank (App E, then the 68 chapter judge boxes by chapter and section); R5 discrepancy and evidence inventories (1.10, 2.11, 3.13, 4.12, 6.13, 7.10, 8.4, 8.7); R6 restated explanations (App B, 4.0).

Reading-time estimates (my assumption, not measured): Part I about 6.3 h at 200 wpm for reading, 3.2 h to skim; adding the knowledge checks and exploration tasks (they are in Part I) roughly doubles that for a reader who does them. The "one hour before a presentation" route in the front matter becomes: App A, App H, 8.11, then R4 "Ten hard questions" (about 3,459 words, 0.3 h).

## 9. Alternatives

**Alternative 1: one book plus "core path" markers (no restructure).** Add a marker (a badge in the heading, or a Core Path list in the front matter) to the 156 Part I sections and leave everything in place. Cost: almost nothing, no broken pointers, one PDF. Benefit: the reader has a route of about 76,113 words. Weakness: the book is still 183 pages and the reference material still interrupts the flow; "concise" is not achieved. It is a useful first step and can be derived from the same `layer_map.json`.

**Alternative 2: keep the book, add an "Executive Companion" of about 12,717 words (assembled from existing text, nothing new written).** Made of: Appendix A (one page) (601); Appendix H (ten concepts + self-test) (2,172); Key ideas to remember (8 chapters) (1,182); What you should now understand (8 chapters) (930); 8.11 Claims you must NOT make (686); Appendix B (architecture explained once) (3,430); 8.3 Architectural limitations + 8.5 Incomplete functionality (1,116); Ten hard questions (App E list) (158); The ten hardest judge boxes (full boxes from Part I) (2,121); Chapter intro sections and 1.5 "in one minute" (321). About 18 pages, 1.1 h. Benefit: the preparation-evening document, with no change to the book. Weakness: it duplicates ~12,717 words by design (it is a view of existing text) and the existing book stays as large as now.

## 10. Recommendation
Choose the two-layer build as the primary format, but ship it in two steps: (1) generate Part I and Part II from the existing sources with `layer_map.json` (this prototype), add "(Part II)" markers to the 10 pointers that cross the boundary and rewrite the front-matter reading routes; (2) only afterwards, and only with the owner's approval, run a separate merge pass over the paraphrased duplicates (Appendix E questions asked twice, Appendix B, the 8.12 table). Keep Alternative 2 as a cheap supplement once Part I exists, since most of its parts are already in Part I. Do not remove anything on the strength of this analysis alone.

## 11. What I could not assess
- Whether the owner experiences a 106-page Part I as concise; whether readers find the stub-and-pointer pattern acceptable; the actual page counts of the two PDFs (not rendered by me; the lead runs the PDF step) and how the typeset tables change the page estimate.
- The correctness of any technical claim in the handbook. This analysis classifies and counts; it does not re-verify claims against the code, and it labels nothing "verified" beyond the counts and the cell-level comparisons the scripts print.
- Content quality of sections I sampled rather than read in full (about 40 sections opened; the rest classified from heading, size and element statistics). Category assignments for 6.11, 8.2, 8.4, 8.7, 3.1, 3.6 and the 1.10 history are the most debatable.
- The effect of moving the 68 judge boxes (plus 2 that sit inside sections that move whole) on exam-style rehearsal; the judge-box selection (4.3) is my judgement and the lead/owner should replace it.
- Cross-reference counts are from a regex and are approximate (they miss pointers phrased in other ways and may mis-attribute a bare "4.3" in a table).
- Anything about the PDF layout (the prototype's PDF step was not run by me).
